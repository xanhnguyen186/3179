AI is transforming the world - so where does Australia stand in this shift?

This visualisation highlights Australia’s AI adoption trends, their connection to students, and the impact on future job opportunities - showing why AI is essential for both study and career readiness.

Is Australia Ready for AI?

Global AI Preparedness Index (2023)

AI Preparedness Index (AIPI) assesses the level of AI preparedness across 174 countries

AI Adoption Rate across Countries (2025)

In 2023, the global map shows Australia scoring highly on AI preparedness, keeping pace with other advanced nations. By 2025, Australia has moved to the top in AI adoption rates, ahead of many major economies. This progression shows that Australia is not only ready for AI but is actively embracing it — an important signal for students preparing for AI-driven careers.

How Has AI Use and Adoption Shifted Across Australian Industries (2023-2024)?

The dumbbell chart depicts that daily active AI users have strongly grown across all Australian industries from 2023 to 2024. Manufacturing, education, and technology lead with over 4 million active users each, while finance and healthcare show smaller but steady growth, showing that AI is becoming a core part of the wider economy. Education’s strong rise highlights how quickly AI is becoming part of student learning and teaching. For them, this growth signals not only a shift in how they learn today but also the kinds of workplaces and careers they will step into tomorrow.

According to the change of AI adoption rate as shown in diverging bars, technology and agriculture recorded the strongest increases. In contrast, finance, manufacturing, healthcare, and especially education saw major declines, suggesting adoption in these areas may already be saturated or slowing. For students, this means that while their sector is already ahead in using AI, future growth may come more from how they deepen and diversify their AI use rather than from new adoption.

How Australian High School Students Are Using AI?

Proportion of Students using AI

The donut chart shows how many students use AI for study compared to those who don’t. A large majority, 81.3%, already use AI tools in their learning, while only 18.8% do not. This highlights that AI has quickly become a mainstream study tool, with most students relying on it for assignments, research, or exam preparation.

AI Tools used by Grades

Click on a tool to see how its use changes by grade

The chart highlights the diversity of AI tools available to students, showing that each tool tends to be used at different stages of study. Some, like Gemini and ChatGPT, are more popular in earlier grades, while tools such as Notion, Educhat, and QuillBot grow in use by Grade 12. This suggests students experiment with a variety of tools over time, relying on different ones as their study needs become more advanced.

AI support in Exam Preparation and Mental Health

This heatmap shows how students use AI during exam preparation at different stress levels. It highlights that students who are on track or fully revised use AI most consistently, and AI use peaks at stress level 10 where a very high proportion rely on it — suggesting that while AI is a steady study aid, it becomes especially crucial when exam pressure is at its highest.

Will AI Create Opportunities or Take Them Away?

Australian AI Job Market and Salary Trends (2024 - 2025)

This chart shows how AI full-time employment annual salaries vary across different industries and experience levels. Each bar represents a salary range in one industry, from entry-level (EN) through mid (MI), senior (SE), and expert (EX). Do AI-Generated Content and Revenue Increases create Job Loss?

Despite the attractive salaries in AI-related fields, the rise of AI-generated content can also lead to job losses. Skills to Stay Competitive in AI Jobs

The word cloud shows the essential skills that make candidates stand out in the competitive AI job market. Core programming tools like Python, frameworks such as TensorFlow and PyTorch, and platforms like Kubernetes dominate, while supporting skills in cloud services, data visualization, and mathematics also play a key role. For students, this means that building a strong mix of technical and analytical skills is crucial to stay competitive and secure high-paying AI opportunities.

Final Words

AI is reshaping the world, and Australia is emerging as one of the leaders in adoption. Industries from technology to agriculture are rapidly expanding their use of AI, while education already shows high uptake, reflecting how deeply AI is integrated into student learning. The real challenge is how students adapt as industries continue to evolve. Most students now rely on AI tools, with preferences shifting as they move through grades and prepare for exams.

At the same time, AI opens up a wide range of job opportunities with attractive salaries, but it also brings risks of job loss in fields heavily affected by automation and AI-generated content. This makes adaptability and continuous skill development critical for students entering the workforce. By embracing AI tools and building key technical skills, students can position themselves not just to cope with change, but to thrive in an AI-driven future.
